#+TITLE: Outcome Reward Modeling Preflight

* Scope
This bounded paper-readiness comparison uses the pinned =Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B=
scalar sequence-classification model, pinned UltraFeedback chosen/rejected
pairs, and the Bradley-Terry log-sigmoid objective. Representax is compared
against TRL 1.10.0 =RewardTrainer= on GPU 2.

The lifecycle run uses seed 7, four updates, global batch 128, microbatch 4,
32 gradient-accumulation steps, BF16 compute, maximum length 1024, and a
midpoint checkpoint/resume after update 2. The deterministic data view has
512 training pairs and 32 held-out pairs. Rows requiring truncation were
excluded; observed lengths are 29--1020 tokens.

* Accepted result
The accepted comparison forces both frameworks to make one =[8, 1024]= Qwen
call per microstep. Each optimizer update therefore processes exactly 262,144
padded tokens in both frameworks. Compilation is retained separately from
warmed throughput.

| Measure | Representax | TRL |
|---------+--------------+-----|
| Warm pairs/s | 7.6965 | 6.6730 |
| Median warm update | 16.6309 s | 19.1740 s |
| Final held-out pairwise accuracy | 0.625 | 0.625 |
| Initial held-out pairwise accuracy | 0.500 | 0.500 |

Representax reaches =1.1534x= TRL warm throughput under equal static shapes.
Its two-process compilation plus first-update time is 50.0726 seconds; this is
reported but excluded from the warm statistic.

Per-update losses are closely aligned:

| Update | Representax | TRL |
|--------+--------------+-----|
|      1 |     0.740731 | 0.731193 |
|      2 |     0.688735 | 0.684397 |
|      3 |     0.743616 | 0.749767 |
|      4 |     0.631849 | 0.644425 |

* Diagnosis and controls
The original =paired-v1= result reported Representax at =0.7620x= TRL. It is
preserved as a rejected diagnostic because its training compute was not
matched. Representax padded the complete global batch to 1024 tokens, while
TRL padded each microbatch only to its longest sequence. Across the first
three updates, TRL therefore processed 183,584, 177,688, and 182,064 padded
tokens instead of 262,144. Dynamic TRL reached 10.1740 pairs/s.

The controlled results separate the causes:

| Control | Representax | TRL | Ratio |
|---------+--------------+-----+-------|
| Static 1024, accumulation 32 | 7.6910 pairs/s | 6.6664 pairs/s | 1.1537x |
| Static 1024, accumulation 1 | 7.3240 pairs/s | 6.5854 pairs/s | 1.1122x |

The direct kernel-only control runs one forward/backward call without an
optimizer or dataloader. Both use rematerialization/gradient checkpointing and
the same =[8, 1024]= inputs:

| Component | Representax | TRL |
|-----------+--------------+-----|
| Qwen forward/backward microstep | 0.5169 s | 0.5845 s |
| Qwen forward/backward pairs/s | 7.7389 | 6.8440 |

The direct native Qwen kernel is =1.1308x= faster, with BF16 losses of 0.804296
and 0.804688. Comparing accumulation 1 and 32 separately estimates the
once-per-update optimizer cost at 0.0269 seconds for Representax and 0.0076
seconds for TRL. The native optimizer update is slower but small and amortized
by accumulation. The observed performance issue was padding scope, not Qwen
kernels or gradient accumulation. Length-grouped global batches remain a
useful future throughput optimization, but are not required for this
equal-compute preflight.

* Correctness and lifecycle
- The pair collator processes chosen and rejected examples together, and the
  reward task/evaluator score one concatenated batch before splitting logits.
- Representax and TRL both stopped at update 2, saved optimizer state, resumed
  in a fresh process, and completed update 4.
- Initial and final held-out evaluation completed for both frameworks.
- Representax native bundle reload preserved scores exactly.
- Representax Hugging Face export loaded as
  =Qwen3ForSequenceClassification= under Transformers with finite scores.
- TRL export/reload preserved scores exactly.
- Verified optimizer checkpoints were discarded after the resume checks;
  final inference exports and compact evidence are retained.

Two generic lifecycle defects found by the first run remain covered by focused
tests: the Qwen reward adapter now works through the generic exporter without
an explicit source argument, and mixed-precision native bundles rebuild FP32
master-parameter templates before loading.
