#+title: Physical ModernVBERT DDP/FSDP frontier — 2026-08-19

* Decision

The named DDP and FSDP plans use the same =build_train_step= API and scientific
program.  On two PCIe-connected RTX 4090s, replicated DDP wins small,
communication-dominated workloads.  Full-model FSDP becomes faster once
long-sequence compute amortizes materialization, while using less live memory.
Layer-boundary FSDP additionally trains a 1.9795B-parameter model after DDP has
already crossed the physical capacity boundary.

This closes the first physical capacity/throughput frontier.  It does not claim
that FSDP is universally faster: the plan expands the feasible-workload set and
its throughput depends on the compute/communication ratio and interconnect.

* Matched scientific program

- two physical RTX 4090s, GPUs 4 and 5, connected through one PCIe host bridge;
- FP32 parameters and compute with highest JAX matmul precision;
- AdamW, learning rate =1e-5=, no weight decay or gradient clipping;
- MNR at scale 20 through exact GradCache, with identity positives;
- deterministic synthetic token tensors, full activation rematerialization;
- one synchronized warmup and three or five synchronized measured updates; and
- =XLA_PYTHON_CLIENT_PREALLOCATE=false=.

The 149M rows load the immutable =ModernVBERT/modernvbert-embed= snapshot.  They
are complete forward/backward/optimizer programs, but deliberately begin at
model-ready tensors and therefore isolate the trainer rather than data quality.

* Full-model frontier

Training tokens count both query and document tokens.  Memory columns are peak
live JAX bytes per GPU, expressed as decimal GB.  Every row is finite and both
devices have active compute and balanced at-rest layouts.

| parameters | global batch | sequence | plan | median step (s) | examples/s | training tokens/s | peak JAX GB | result |
|------------+--------------+----------+------+-----------------+------------+-------------------+-------------+--------|
| 149,044,992 | 8 | 128 | DDP | 0.1783 | 44.87 | 11,485 | 5.987 | completed |
| 149,044,992 | 8 | 128 | full FSDP | 1.0441 | 7.66 | 1,962 | 4.696 | completed |
| 149,044,992 | 8 | 512 | DDP | 0.4480 | 17.86 | 18,285 | 6.157 | completed |
| 149,044,992 | 8 | 512 | full FSDP | 1.1833 | 6.76 | 6,923 | 4.799 | completed |
| 149,044,992 | 2 | 4096 | DDP | 2.5310 | 0.790 | 6,473 | 10.022 | completed |
| 149,044,992 | 2 | 4096 | full FSDP | 1.8774 | 1.065 | 8,727 | 8.590 | completed |

At sequence 4096, full-model FSDP is 34.8% faster in training-token throughput,
uses 14.3% less peak live JAX memory, and 23.9% less peak process memory than
DDP.  At sequence 128 and 512, communication dominates and DDP is materially
faster.  The first measured losses agree within =2e-6= at all three points; the
sequence-4096 trajectories remain within =8.6e-6= after three updates.

The two-device compiler emits 10 AllGathers and 10 AllReduces for the small
full-FSDP shapes, versus the accepted four-device packed schedule of 2 and 6.
That topology-sensitive schedule explains the small-workload trough and remains
a future compiler-plan optimization, not a hidden correctness exception.

* Capacity frontier

The largest successful capacity configuration is
=benchmarks/configs/modernvbert-capacity-2.0b.json=.  Its exact parameter count
is 1,979,520,768.  Its FP32 trainable arrays plus Adam moments require at least
23,754,249,216 bytes per GPU under DDP before gradients, activations, or an
output state.  The smaller 1,910,506,752-parameter configuration already OOMs
under DDP with the same CUDA-async allocator.

| plan | allocator | at-rest model/GPU | at-rest Adam/GPU | peak JAX/GPU | median step | result |
|------+-----------+-------------------+------------------+--------------+-------------+--------|
| DDP, 1.9105B | JAX CUDA async | 7.642 GB | 15.284 GB | — | — | OOM requesting 21.21 GiB result state |
| layer FSDP, 1.9105B | JAX CUDA async | 3.821 GB | 7.642 GB | 20.300 GB | 12.024 s | completed |
| layer FSDP, 1.9795B | JAX CUDA async | 3.959 GB | 7.918 GB | 20.995 GB | 12.524 s | completed |

The successful layer-FSDP runs use global batch 2, sequence 128, buffer donation,
one warmup, and three measured updates.  At 1.9795B, both devices store exactly
3,959,041,563 model bytes and 7,918,083,076 Adam bytes, reach 100% sampled
utilization, and complete finite non-skipped updates.  Its compiled HLO contains
8 AllGathers, 5 AllReduces, and 4 ReduceScatters.

The default BFC allocator could not find the required 8.23 GiB contiguous live
workspace at this boundary.  =XLA_PYTHON_CLIENT_ALLOCATOR=cuda_async= completes
the identical program.  The ignored TensorFlow variable =TF_GPU_ALLOCATOR= is
retained only in the raw failed artifact as diagnostic provenance.

The tested upper bracket is 2,324,590,880 parameters.  Its layer-FSDP run OOMs
on a 10.13 GiB live allocation, so the evidence claims a demonstrated feasible
point, not that every model whose sharded persistent state fits will train.

* Compilation and communication evidence

Compilation is reported separately from steady-state time.  The three 149M
DDP/FSDP pairs compile in 18.6/19.2, 30.9/20.1, and 27.8/30.6 seconds.  The
successful capacity retries compile from cache in approximately 0.86 seconds
after the failed allocator trials, so that number is not a cold-compile
comparison.

Every successful raw artifact records placement, lowering, compilation,
per-update timing, losses, logical and addressable layouts, JAX and NVML memory,
physical device IDs, and StableHLO/compiled-HLO collective counts.  Raw evidence
lives under =/raid/representax/benchmarks/fsdp-frontier-20260819/=.  Reproduce a
point with =benchmarks/profile_sharding_modernvbert.py=; failed processes also
write a structured =status="oom"= artifact before returning nonzero.
